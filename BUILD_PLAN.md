# Build Plan

How we get from "locked phone and no tree" to a LineageOS build for the Moto g 2026 (`utah`, XT2613-1). Status of each phase is tracked here. See [README.md](README.md) for the device facts.

## Where we left off (end of 2026-11-18, evening): START HERE

**One line:** after a day of flashing debug builds, our LineageOS 23 for the Moto g 2026 (`utah`) **boots to the boot animation with Android's framework alive** (USB MTP appears, `zygote`, `surfaceflinger`, `system_server` run) **but the whole phone reboots about every 6 to 7 minutes and never finishes booting**. It is wired in, **built and checked on the laptop (release `utah-20261118-debug8`, see "RESULT OF THAT BUILD" below), but NOT yet flashed or booted.**

Nothing of ours should be on it; **check `fastboot getvar current-slot`, `is-userspace` and the slot state before assuming anything**, and re-read "Bootloader flashing rules learned" below before any flash. No flashing happened after the last debug7 test.

**What the last build was (laptop, 18:02, `UTAH_DEBUG_PANIC=1 DEVICE=utah JOBS=10`, tab "Laptop build 8"):** the stock vendor image is staged by `scripts/make-utah-vendor.sh` (copies `~/lineage/work/parts/vendor_a.img` to `vendor/motorola/utah/prebuilt/vendor.img`, not in git); `trees/device_motorola_utah/BoardConfig.mk` sets `BOARD_PREBUILT_VENDORIMAGE` to it; the debug logger moved from the vendor to `/system` (`device.mk`). **First things to do next time:**
1. **The build is done and checked** (see "RESULT OF THAT BUILD"): nothing to rebuild unless the tree changes. If you rebuild, `build-live` says how it ended; for a failure the likely places are the vbmeta/AVB handling of a prebuilt vendor, the VINTF check against our own (now unused) vendor directory, or SELinux file-context steps.
2. **For any new build run `scripts/make-release.sh <name>` on the laptop** (the ready release is `utah-20261118-debug8`): it checks the logger files in `/system`, that `out/.../vendor.img` is byte-identical to stock's, the HIDL service manager pieces in `system_ext`, and the boot image headers; then `scripts\fetch-release.ps1 <name>` on the PC.
3. **Expect these to behave differently now**, because the vendor is stock's: `ro.zygote=zygote64` and every `ro.vendor.*` come from stock; the stock audio, thermal, composer, Bluetooth and MediaTek multimedia HALs run with their own matching libraries; Lineage's vendor extras (touch HAL, Lineage health, Pixel power profiles) are gone. The SELinux policy is a mix of Lineage's system policy and stock's vendor policy (precompiled policy hash will not match, so init compiles it at boot with `secilc`; permissive for now).
4. **Then the flash procedure that worked all day** (details and the traps are in "Flash day, 2026-11-18" under "Boot log findings"): our hand-made footer-less recovery `vendor_boot` on slot `a`; `fastboot reboot recovery`; Apply update > Apply from ADB; `adb sideload` (installs into `b`; the client stops at ~47% and the phone asks to reboot to recovery, say yes); Power + Volume Down to fastboot; check `current-slot: b`; flash stock's `vendor_boot` with the debug command line (`make-vendor-boot-cmdline.py` on `D:\LineageOS\firmware\...\vendor_boot.img`, args `androidboot.selinux=permissive ignore_loglevel printk.devkmsg=on panic=3`) to `vendor_boot_b`; reboot; the stock "No command" screen is cleared with Power + Volume Up > Reboot system now; wait, force the phone off, fastboot, `set_active a`, `fastboot reboot recovery`, Advanced > Enable ADB, mount `/dev/block/by-name/metadata` read-only and pull `/dbg` and `/dbg.prev`. **Never sideload from a recovery running on slot `b` (target `a`): it left slot `a` in a loop and slot `b`'s recovery hanging.** Images made on 2026-11-18 are in `D:\LineageOS\release\utah-20261118-debug*` (`vendor_boot_recovery.img`, `vendor_boot_stock_debug.img`).
5. **Open problem that survives this change: our own build's `vendor_boot` hangs silently at the logo** (no kernel panic, no USB, whatever the command line), while stock's `vendor_boot` with the rest of our build boots into Android. Stock's platform ramdisk differs from ours in having `/init`, a full recovery tree, `snapuserd` and its own first-stage binaries; ours relies on `init_boot` and has only `first_stage_ramdisk` + `lib`. The DTB table and the fstab were ruled out. Until this is fixed every test needs the stock-based `vendor_boot` flashed by hand after each install.
6. Still unexplained from the HAL-by-HAL days (may disappear with the stock vendor): `bluetooth-1-1` and `vendor.thermal-hal-2-0.mtk` exited silently after ~30 ms; nine MediaTek multimedia binaries/libs had unresolved symbols (`mnld`, `mtkconverter`, `mmagent`, `mmlpq`, `pq_aidl`, two codec2 libs, the neural-networks shim).

**What we learned today (so nobody repeats it):** the crash-log method that finally worked is a logger running on the phone that writes into **`/metadata/dbg`** (unencrypted, readable from our recovery after any restart or forced power-off); the panic-timer and "hold a key through the restart" methods cost hours. The tooling added: `build-live` (live display, now also reports how a build ended: finished, failed, killed for memory by systemd-oomd or by the kernel, machine restarted, stopped by hand, and "no contact with the laptop"), `scripts/make-release.sh` + `scripts/fetch-release.ps1` (release set laptop -> PC with checksums), `scripts/check-vendor-libs.sh` and `scripts/check-vendor-symbols.py` (built for the HAL-by-HAL approach: strict "can vendor code load this library / resolve this symbol" checks of a built image; keep them, they find the "Android 13 blob vs Android 16 library" class of mismatch), `build-nevada.sh` now opts its scope out of `systemd-oomd` and clears stale failed units, and `--state=running` fixes in `build-live`. Rules learned about the build system: ELF files must never go through `PRODUCT_COPY_FILES` (use `cc_prebuilt_*` modules); a file name that Soong also installs collides ("overriding commands for target"); a child makefile cannot remove packages it inherits (patch the parent: `patches/device_motorola_nevada/0004`); a process in `/vendor/bin` can only load libraries from `/vendor/lib64` and the LL-NDK list, never from `/system/lib64`; same-named libraries with the same exported function names can still differ in struct layout, which no symbol check sees.

**Bootloader flashing rules learned (this phone, 2026-11-17):** (a) "Preflash validation failed" means an image **with an AVB footer does not match the `vbmeta` of the CURRENT slot** (`fastboot getvar current-slot`); images without a footer are always accepted (strip it with `scripts/make-vendor-boot-cmdline.py`, or for `init_boot` zero the bytes after the content). So: to flash stock images, first flash stock `vbmeta`/`vbmeta_system` to the current slot; to flash ours, flash our `vbmeta` first. (b) `fastboot reboot recovery` leaves a recovery request behind. (c) A forced power-off wipes the kernel log. (d) `--disable-verity --disable-verification` on `vbmeta` was blocked by the safety rules; not needed. (e) Never touch `nvdata`, `nvcfg`, `persist`, `proinfo`, `lk`, `preloader`, `tee`, the modem or the GPT; Motorola's own rescue sequence (`flashfile.xml`) erases `nvdata`, which we deliberately do not replay.

### Memory (lesson from 2026-11-15)

At 20:01 on 2026-11-15 the kernel's out-of-memory killer stopped a build. `soong_build`, the analysis step that runs before any compiling, had reached **about 28 GB of RAM** (`anon-rss:28468104kB` in `journalctl -k`). With 29 GiB of RAM and 8 GiB of swap, nothing was left. Because the build had been started from a Claude session, systemd counted it as part of the Claude app's process group (`app-com.anthropic.Claude-*.scope`), so **the Claude app was killed too**.

Rules for future builds:
- **Swap: 40 GiB.** [scripts/grow-swap.sh](scripts/grow-swap.sh) resizes `/swap.img` in place (`sudo scripts/grow-swap.sh`, or `SIZE=48G` for more). `/etc/fstab` already points at `/swap.img`, so the new size survives reboots. Check with `swapon --show`. Status: script written, **not run yet** (it needs `sudo`).
- **Run every build in its own systemd scope**, so that running out of memory kills only the build, never the terminal or the Claude app. [scripts/build-nevada.sh](scripts/build-nevada.sh) now does this itself: it re-runs inside `systemd-run --user --scope` as a unit named `lineage-build-<timestamp>`, capped at `MemoryMax=26G` and `MemorySwapMax=40G` (change with `MEM_MAX=` / `SWAP_MAX=`, skip with `NO_SCOPE=1`). Tested 2026-11-15: the scope sits outside the Claude app's group, and the caps apply. Any build run by hand with `m`/`mka` should be wrapped the same way.
- Close browsers and other heavy apps while `soong_build` runs (the first few minutes). Lowering `-j` does not help with this step, because `soong_build` is a single process. It only helps during the compile that follows.
- If a build dies with no error in `~/lineage/build.log`, check `journalctl -k | grep -i "killed process"` first.

### Whole-laptop power-off (2026-11-15 22:03)

The 4th build (attempt 4, at 57%, step 112,853 of 197,761, about 1h35m in) ended because **the whole laptop switched off**. It was not a build error and not out of memory.
- The journal for that boot just stops at 22:03:41. Messages were still arriving up to that second, and there is no shutdown sequence. The next boot came up at 22:03:59. `~/lineage/build.log` ends in null bytes, the usual sign of a hard power cut.
- The next boot's kernel log records why (AMD CPUs report this): `Previous system reset reason [0x00200800]: ACPI power state transition occurred`. A normal reboot shows `software wrote 0x6 to reset control register 0xCF9` instead. So the firmware cut the power. It was **not** the CPU's own thermal trip, a 4-second power-button press, a machine-check or data-fabric error (each has its own code), a kernel panic, or an OOM kill.
- On mains power with the battery full, and no power-supply events in the log.
- Most likely cause: **the embedded controller (EC) of the MSI Creator A16 AI+ shutting down for heat**, after about 1.5 h at 93% CPU on the `performance` profile (5 GHz). Not proven: no temperatures were logged during the build. Signs that point to heat: at idle the SSD's Sensor 1 already reads 64 °C, the BIOS is old (`E15FKAMS.309`, 2024-09-20), `thermald` does not support this CPU, and the MSI fan sensors read 0 RPM, so Linux cannot see or control the fans.
- Not the cause: the steady `AER ... BadDLLP/BadTLP` messages come from the Realtek 2.5GbE port (`00:02.2`). They appear on every boot, idle ones too, and are correctable.
- Rules until this is understood: log temperatures during builds (k10temp `Tctl`, nvme, acpitz), so the next crash shows what heated up. Prefer the `balanced` power profile (`powerprofilesctl set balanced`) or a lower `-j` for long builds. Keep the vents clear and the laptop raised. Check MSI for a newer BIOS/EC firmware. Ninja resumes where it stopped, so a crash costs only the time to restart.

**Aftermath: a power cut leaves corrupt build outputs (found 2026-11-15 22:41).** The restart at 22:14 failed after 5 minutes in `adbd` with `undefined symbol` errors from protobuf. The real cause was `libprotobuf-cpp-lite.a`, which was **0 bytes**, stamped 22:03. The crash lost data that was not yet flushed to disk. About 12,400 files in `out/` were written in the last minutes before the cut (644 of them empty), and Ninja treats a present file as up to date, so it kept using the broken ones. The `build.log` full of null bytes was the visible sign of the same problem.
- **After any hard crash or power cut, delete every output written in the last few minutes before it**, then restart. Ninja rebuilds what is missing, and ccache keeps this cheap. Run from `~/lineage/android/out`, with the right times: `find . -type f -newermt "<crash time minus 10 min>" ! -newermt "<crash time plus 1 min>" ! -name ".ninja_*" ! -name "*.log" -delete`. Done 2026-11-15 for 21:55–22:05 (23,079 files).
- A link error about undefined symbols from a library that is on the link line, in a build that previously passed that step, points to a 0-byte or truncated `.a`/`.o`. Check with `ls -la` and `llvm-ar t`.

*Lessons from this attempt*
- Before any flash, **prove the USB link with a large transfer**, not just `getvar`. (Small commands and an 8-16 MB write worked right up until the 64 MiB ones.) A short, known-data USB-A to USB-C cable on a USB-A port is the safest.
- **Never flash `vbmeta` before the images it covers.** Do the big images first, `vbmeta` last. Here `vbmeta` was flashed first and was already in place when the big writes failed.
- The plan to flash our images onto the **current** slot `_b` (so sideload writes the full system to `_a`, the way the LineageOS guide does it) means the only known-good slot is the one being modified. Next time, if a better safety net is wanted, check `_a` first by booting it once.
- fastboot 34.0.5 (Debian) fails with `Error reading sparse file` on `-S` chunks of these images. `img2simg` sparse files did not help (fastboot expanded them back to 64 MiB). The AOSP-built `fastboot` 36.0.0 (`~/lineage/android/out/host/linux-x86/bin/fastboot`) failed the same way, because the link itself was failing.
- The user decided (2026-11-17) not to back up the IMEI/`nvdata`/`persist` partitions.

### Tracked issue: partitions our build does not create (opened 2026-11-17)

**The `system_dlkm` workaround is a stopgap, not a fix.** Our `super` is missing a logical partition that stock has, and we are currently getting past that by deleting its lines from the fstab ([scripts/drop-system-dlkm-from-fstab.sh](scripts/drop-system-dlkm-from-fstab.sh)). That hides the error; it does not give the phone what the partition held. **Expect this to bite later** (missing drivers, odd module-load failures after the first boot), so do not forget it once the phone "boots".

Facts so far:
- Stock `super` (from our firmware): `product`, `system`, `system_dlkm`, `system_ext`, `vendor`, `vendor_dlkm`, all EROFS, no `odm`. Ours: the same minus **`system_dlkm`**. So it is the only partition we know to be missing, but check the full list again after any change (`lpdump`/the LP table, and `ab_partitions.txt`).
- `system_dlkm` is the GKI "dynamically loadable kernel modules" partition: kernel modules for the generic kernel part (`/system_dlkm/lib/modules`), loaded after first-stage init. Stock's copy is the image `system_dlkm_a` on the laptop in `~/lineage/work/parts/` (split out of our firmware's `super` earlier), and its EROFS contents are in `~/lineage/work/x/system_dlkm/` if that unpack was kept.
- Where it is required: first-stage init (`first_stage_ramdisk/fstab.mt6835`, `first_stage_mount`) and `vold` (`/vendor/etc/fstab.mt6835`); both aborted when it was missing. Stock marks it with plain `avb` (not `vbmeta_system`), like `vendor_dlkm`.
- The 197 modules in our `vendor_ramdisk` and the vendor_dlkm ones are already byte-identical to stock, so the kernel side is fine for first boot.
- **Inspected 2026-11-17 (Windows PC, from the firmware's `super` chunks; image saved as `D:\LineageOS\work\system_dlkm_a.img`, 348,160 bytes = 0.35 MB):** stock's `system_dlkm` holds **no kernel modules at all**. Then make sure it is in the super partition list (`BOARD_*_PARTITION_LIST`), fits the super group size, is in the OTA partition list (`AB_OTA_PARTITIONS`), and is covered by vbmeta (hash tree), or `ab_partitions.txt` and the zip will not match what the fstab expects.
3. **Alternative to building it:** ship stock's `system_dlkm_a.img` unchanged as a prebuilt image in the zip (same kernel, same modules, so it is consistent). Simplest if the build allows a prebuilt partition image.
4. **Then undo the workaround:** remove the `drop-system-dlkm-from-fstab.sh` step from the rebuild flow and put the lines back, so the tree matches stock again.
5. **Compare the other fstab entries too** (`/mnt/vendor/*`, `protect1/2`, `nvdata`, `nvcfg`, `persist`, `tzapp_a`, `logks`): they are not first-stage required (except `logks`, `nofail`), but vold or later init stages may need them, and a missing or differently named partition shows up the same way.

Related things seen in the same boot log that may be the same kind of gap (check them together): `hwservicemanager` not found, the vendor property denials, and `/vendor/bin/init.oem.hw.sh` having no SELinux domain transition. They point at the Android 16 system and the Android 13 vendor not matching, which is the "vendor mismatch" risk below.

## Risks to keep in mind

- **Bricking.** A bad flash can leave the phone unusable. A MediaTek phone can often be recovered with stock firmware, but we should not assume it. Test with `fastboot boot` first and keep the phone you depend on as a spare if possible.
- **Locked-down carrier model.** XT2613-1 is the US carrier variant (retus). Motorola's unlock portal may refuse it even though the OEM unlocking toggle is available. We find out when we request the code.
- **Calls and mobile data** on MediaTek phones depend on proprietary IMS pieces; they are the most common failure area.
- **The `nevada` tree is unproven.** It reportedly compiles, but we have no evidence it boots.
- **Android 16 vendor mismatch.** The phone's vendor side is Android 13 based, so the tree and blobs must match it.
